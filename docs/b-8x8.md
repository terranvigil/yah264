# B_8x8: correct, -1.27% alone, and -1.93% with the rectangular modes it unlocks

B_8x8 coding is the prerequisite for the B-frame quality track. x264 codes 6.4%
of its B macroblocks that way, and derives from the same analysis the
early-terminate estimates that make its rectangular partitions affordable. This
is the build.

**Status: shipped on by default (`Y264_B_8X8=1`, with `Y264_B8_RATE=1` and
`Y264_B8_QGATE=10`), fully recon-verified, conformance 254/254 with the mode
armed.** Corrected 2026-09-03: the mode was default OFF when this was written,
so the untouched-default-path checks here (byte-identical against a pre-change
binary on foreman, `make test` 9/9) describe the pre-ship default. The
rectangular partitions B_8x8 unlocks are still off at the medium tier
(`Y264_B_RECT`) and kept at subme >= 9.

## What is implemented

Four independently predicted 8x8 quadrants, each choosing among the standard's
four sub_mb_types (B_Direct_8x8 / B_L0_8x8 / B_L1_8x8 / B_Bi_8x8), at 8x8
granularity only. The 8x4/4x8/4x4 sub-partitions are out of scope, as they are
for x264 at medium.

- per-quadrant search (`search_b_8x8`), seeded from the 16x16 winner per list
  and pinned to its list-0 reference. A cold search over every reference is
  what makes these modes unaffordable;
- prediction (`build_b8_pred`) and motion commit (`commit_b8_motion`), both
  reading the result alone. A quadrant's DERIVED direct motion and reference
  are stored into the result so nothing downstream reaches back for the
  per-macroblock direct field, which is only valid during analysis while the
  CABAC emit runs in a later pass;
- CAVLC (`mb_type` 22 plus four `sub_mb_type`, refs and mvds, 7.3.5.2 order)
  and CABAC (mb_type's five 1-bins at contexts 30/31/32, `sub_mb_type` per
  9.3.2.5, ref and mvd), and hence the RD bit estimate, which routes through
  the same emitter.

`Y264_B8_DIRECT=0` excludes the direct quadrants; that is the subset the mode
was first verified on, kept as an A/B control.

## Four bugs found, all real

**The winner re-encode did not know the mode.** `analyze_b_mb`'s S4 stage
rebuilds the winning prediction and routed every `bpart != 0` to
`build_bpart_pred`, which only understands the two-partition splits. A winning
B_8x8 had its residual re-encoded against a garbage prediction. Ten of twelve
frames mismatched; fixing it left one.

**The ref_idx context read a hard-coded zero for direct neighbours.** Inside
the macroblock, a neighbour quadrant's `condTermFlag` needs its list-0
reference, which for a `B_Direct_8x8` quadrant is derived, not coded.

**And then it read the derived value, which is also wrong.** That was the
"desyncs one frame in twelve" bug. `SubMbPredMode(B_Direct_8x8)` is Direct, so
`predModeEqualFlag` is 0 in 9.3.3.1.1.6 and the flag is **0 for a direct
neighbour whatever its reference**; x264 keeps the same rule, tracking which
quadrants are direct in a per-macroblock bitmap. The derived refIdxL0 does sit
in our motion grid and does exceed 0 once list 0 has more than one entry, which
is why the failure needed `--ref > 1` and mixed sub-types and nothing else.
Both the in-macroblock neighbour and the neighbouring-macroblock case were
wrong; the second needs the
per-quadrant direct bitmap, now carried in `mbcbp` bits 24-27.

This is a CABAC-context bug, so it cannot show under CAVLC. An earlier bisect
placed it in "the shared reconstruction path, not the syntax, because it fails
at the same macroblock under both coders" while reading stale files: in zsh a
config held in a scalar does not word-split, so the CAVLC arm never ran the
config it printed, and the harness compared the previous iteration's leftovers.

**`b8_blk_pred` overflowed its own buffers.** `y264_me_mc_luma` hard-codes a
**stride-16** destination on both of its paths. `b8_blk_pred` passed it
`pixel[64]` buffers and read them back at stride 8, so each 8x8 prediction
wrote 120 pixels into 64, scribbled the caller's stack, and ranked the four
sub_mb_types on garbage for seven of the eight rows. This is what made the mode
non-deterministic run to run under threads (five thread-dependent conformance
failures). TSan is silent on it: it is a stack overflow, not a race, and the
tell was that run-to-run variance survived every stair knob being turned off.

## The RD screen

The candidate passes the same SATD screen as every other one in the B
tournament: `search_b_8x8` returns the summed per-quadrant cost (SATD plus
mv/ref rate, commensurate with `satd16[]` and `ptsatd[]`) and the RD trial is
skipped when that sum is outside `thresh` of the tournament's best SATD.

It is nearly inert, and that is itself a result: the quadrant split lands
within 6% of the best 16x16-level SATD on most B macroblocks, which is the same
fact as the mode winning often. It is worth 1-3 points of wall on three of five
table cells and **free in quality**: band median -1.29% without the screen,
-1.27% with, all 12 clips negative in both.

## The second prize: B_RECT is no longer refused

x264 does not search 16x8/8x16 cold. It **estimates** each rectangular
partition from the 8x8 quadrants it has already analysed (the better of the two
quadrants' L0 / L1 / Bi SATD sums, charged the average of their motion rates,
since one partition codes one motion vector per list where two quadrants code
two) and searches the split only when that estimate already beats the best cost
so far. Four cold searches per macroblock is exactly what got our rectangular
modes turned off.

`search_b_8x8` therefore runs BEFORE the rectangular one on the threshold path
and hands back its per-quadrant per-list SATDs and rates, which build the two
estimates. The three arms on the CRF band, with wall from an interleaved A/B
harness at t18:

| arm | BD median | clips better | wall |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| B_8x8 alone | **-1.27%** | 12/12 | 5.4-12.3% |
| + B_RECT, searched cold | **-2.33%** | 11/12 | 18.0-36.6% |
| + B_RECT, estimate-gated | **-1.93%** | 12/12 | **9.6-20.5%** |

The estimates give up 0.4 points of BD and take back roughly half the wall. The
two modes are strongly super-additive: separately they are worth -1.27% and
-0.79%, together -1.93%. With B_8x8 present the rectangular modes add 0.66
points of BD for about 8 points of wall, where alone they cost 26-38% for 0.79.

**One trap in the wiring.** The estimate gate wants the running best cost
INCLUDING B_8x8 (x264's cost), but the RD screen's `isi` must not: folding
`sum8` into `isi` tightens `thresh` wherever B_8x8 has the best SATD and screens
out 16x16 candidates that go on to win RD. Measured, that one line took the
B_8x8 band from -1.27% to **-0.81%**. Two jobs, two variables.

## The skip-exit reorder

The quadrant search runs AFTER the 16x16 RD phase and the B_SKIP exit, which is
x264's order exactly: b16x16, the direct-competitiveness test, the first
the B RD stage, the B_SKIP return, and only then the B 8x8 analysis.
Running the search in the SATD phase instead threw away a fifth to a third of
it, since x264's B_SKIP return sits before the quadrant analysis and ours did
not.

`Y264_B8_STAT`, before and after the reorder:

| clip | search population | wasted by the skip exit |
|---|--:|--:|
| foreman, before | 51.6% of B MBs | 19.1% of searches |
| foreman, after | **42.7%** | **0** |
| park_joy, before | -- | 30.2% of searches |
| park_joy, after | **28.2%** | **0** |

The 16x16 RD screen is now the 16x16 modes' own, the best of direct / L0 / L1 /
Bi, which is what x264's cost is at its first B RD stage, instead of
folding the rectangular SATDs in, which only ever tightened it.

**Quality is neutral across the reorder.** CRF band on the gated arm, all
twelve clips: 12/12 negative, median **-1.39%**. The default path is
byte-identical by construction and by md5: with both B_8x8 and B_RECT off the
rectangular SATDs never entered the screen, so the two thresholds are the same
number.

**The reorder's own wall is null to about +1%** (foreman +0.10%, samsung
+0.04%, park_joy +0.39%, against controls of +0.97%, -0.37% and -1.19%).
Smaller than "19-30% of the search half" suggests, and the reason is visible in
hindsight: the macroblocks the skip exit removes are the CHEAP ones. They leave
because direct is already competitive, which is the same condition under which
their eight quadrant searches converge fastest. The reorder's value is that it
is x264's shape and that the work it removes is provably discarded, not that it
is fast.

## Where the wall goes: `Y264_B8_STAT`

Default-inert counters, t1 (plain globals, so read them single-threaded like
`Y264_BPROF`). At CRF 27, with the search in the SATD phase:

| clip | B MBs | search runs | RD trial | trial beat best | search wasted by the skip exit |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| foreman | 60031 | **100%** | 77.4% | 4.5% | **19.1%** |
| mobile | 65968 | **100%** | 78.0% | 11.1% | **19.2%** |
| park_joy | 1128110 | **100%** | 69.1% | 11.1% | **30.2%** |

Three readings, each naming a lever. The quadrant search runs on **every** B
macroblock that reaches the SATD phase. The RD screen admits **seven

by the mid-tournament skip exit, which the reorder above removes.

## Two knobs, both now default on

Corrected 2026-09-03: both were inert when this was written and both ship armed,
`Y264_B8_RATE=1` and `Y264_B8_QGATE=10`. Read the settings below as what the
encoder does, not as arms to add.

**`Y264_B8_RATE=1`** charges the B_8x8 side of the mb_type / sub_mb_type rate in
the SATD domain (9 for the split's own type, 1/3/3/5 for direct / L0 / L1 / Bi
quadrants, the same charges x264 makes) without touching the 16x16 and direct
costs the way `Y264_BMB_COST` does. It also charges the rectangular types on
their **estimate**, which is the half that is not optional: arm the rest alone
and the estimate becomes the only candidate in the comparison carrying no
side-information term, so the
admitted rectangular searches go *up* (foreman 30004 -> 43114). With the charge
they go down, to 23008. RD trials fall from 77.4% to 59.9% (foreman) and 78.0%
to 70.8% (mobile).

**`Y264_B8_QGATE=<n>`** spends four `satd8x8` on the winning 16x16 prediction to
decide whether the eight searches are worth running at all. A split can only pay
where the quadrants want different motion, and the residual's spread across the
four quadrants is that signal, already in hand. Admit when

    qmax * 4 * 16 > qsum * (16 + n)

i.e. when the worst quadrant carries more than `1 + n/16` of its even share. A
macroblock the gate declines declines the **rectangular** searches too. It has
to: without the 8x8 analysis their estimates do not exist and they would run
cold, which is the form that priced them at 26-38%. Before the reorder, n = 4
ran the search on 51.6% (foreman) / 58.4% (mobile) of B macroblocks and the RD
trial on 31.6% / 41.5%.

## The knee: seven arms on the CRF band, twelve clips each

BD is deterministic, so these are solid whatever the box is doing; the
engagement columns come from `Y264_B8_STAT` on foreman at t1 and are
deterministic too.

| arm | BD median | negative | search runs on | RD trial on |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| B_8x8 + B_RECT | **-2.00%** | 12/12 | 80.7% | 59.3% |
| + `QGATE=2` | -1.50% | 12/12 | 68.6% | 50.2% |
| + `QGATE=4` | -1.17% | 11/12 | 42.7% | 31.3% |
| + `RATE` | -1.85% | 12/12 | 80.7% | 59.3% |
| + `RATE` + `QGATE=2` | **-1.82%** | 11/12 | 68.6% | 50.2% |
| + `RATE` + `QGATE=4` | -1.39% | 12/12 | 42.7% | 31.3% |
| + `RATE` + `QGATE=6` | -0.96% | 11/12 | 24.1% | 17.7% |

The table stops at 6 and the ship is **`Y264_B8_QGATE=10`**, added 2026-09-03.
10 is the knee that was taken: against 6 it costs 1.09-1.34% of t1 wall instead
of 1.99-3.94%, for about half the BD gain, and it keeps that gain on bus and
stefan, the two clips carrying goal 1's quality deficit. 6 overshoots, it
delivers 0.15 dVMAF where goal 1 needed 0.11 and charges the difference to the
median-speed metric. 16 is past the knee, foreman goes positive there.

**The two knobs interact, and that is the result.** On its own the quadrant
gate costs half a BD point at its gentlest setting (-2.00% -> -1.50%). With the
rate charge armed it costs **three hundredths** (-1.85% -> -1.82%). The
mechanism is the half of `Y264_B8_RATE` that is easy to skip: the rectangular
types' own charge on their estimate. A gated macroblock loses its 8x8 analysis
and therefore its rectangular estimates, and without
that charge the surviving candidate set is the one comparison in the tournament
with no side-information term in it. Charge it and the gate is nearly free; do
not, and the gate throws quality away.

So the arm has two sensible settings:

- **maximum quality: `Y264_B_8X8=1 Y264_B_RECT=1`**, -2.00% on 12 of 12;
- **best value: add `Y264_B8_RATE=1 Y264_B8_QGATE=2`**, -1.82%, with the
  quadrant search on 68.6% of B macroblocks instead of 80.7% and the RD trial
  on 50.2% instead of 59.3%.

Measure several arms in ONE interleaved batch. Measuring the knee one
invocation at a time returns it non-monotonic in the gate's own strength.

## The price, against x264's price for the same modes

Dropping `b8x8` from x264's `--partitions` turns off exactly this arm: x264
wraps the B 8x8 analysis, the 16x8/8x16 estimates AND
the B 16x8 and 8x16 analyses behind one sub-16x16 flag,
so `--partitions p8x8,i8x8,i4x4` is their version of our default and
`p8x8,b8x8,i8x8,i4x4` is their version of our arm. Both sides on a fair
no-asm-autovec build, t18, medians of 5, duplicate-base control, every timed
run to `/dev/null`:

| cell | bare arm | + `RATE` + `QGATE=2` | + `RATE` + `QGATE=4` | **x264's own price** |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| foreman | 12.10% | 10.09% | **6.21%** | 6.93% |
| bus | 11.26% | 9.20% | **6.42%** | 4.57% |
| ducks | 17.28% | 13.24% | **6.66%** | 16.56% (VOID) |
| park_joy | 18.29% | 13.56% | **8.40%** | 10.55% |
| samsung | 11.31% | 7.72% | **4.77%** | 6.07% |

As a ratio of x264's price for the identical feature:

| arm | foreman | bus | park_joy | samsung |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| bare | 1.75 | 2.46 | 1.73 | 1.86 |
| `RATE` + `QGATE=2` | 1.46 | 2.01 | 1.29 | 1.27 |
| `RATE` + `QGATE=4` | **0.90** | 1.40 | **0.80** | **0.79** |

**The modes are expensive for everyone: x264 pays 4.6-10.6% for them.** The
gap is not resolution-shaped; the ratio is flat across CIF and 720p, and the
worst cell is bus, which is CIF. At `RATE` + `QGATE=4` the arm costs what x264
costs, 0.79-1.40x, for **-1.39% BD on 12 of 12 clips**. At `QGATE=2` it is
-1.82% for 1.27-2.01x. So this is a genuine speed/quality choice at roughly the
same exchange rate x264 gets, not an implementation defect.

**Two traps in this measurement.** A harness that times the bitstream write
inflates the BASELINE, so the arm's cost as a percentage comes out too small,
and most so where the bitstream is biggest: that alone manufactured a
"resolution-shaped gap" that does not exist. And a box running a desktop video
process cannot time a 720p cell at all: the same park_joy encode to `/dev/null`
read 1.63, 1.93, 4.28 and 5.24 s within minutes while the CIF cells held to a
1.03 spread.

## `QGATE=6`, gated whole

`Y264_B_8X8=1 Y264_B_RECT=1 Y264_B8_RATE=1 Y264_B8_QGATE=6` against the
then-current defaults, which had all four off. The flip to 6 was refused and 10
ships, so read this as the pricing that settled the gate, not as the shipped
arm.

| gate | result |
|---|---|
| CRF band, twelve clips | **-0.74% median, 12/12 negative**, worst -0.06% |
| ABR band, twelve clips | -0.53% median, 8/12 negative |
| `recon_sweep.sh` | 300/300 |
| `determ_repeat.sh` under six spinners | 2/2 over 12 runs each |

The ABR band's four positives split three to one against each clip's own noise
floor: touchdown +3.87% (floor 3.60-12.33), park_joy +0.52% (0.51-1.39) and
akiyo +0.04% (1.09-2.87) are inside their floors and are not results, but
**ducks +1.01% against a floor of 0.07-0.20 is outside its floor and is a real
ABR regression** on a table clip. Recorded, not resolved.

Priced on the table's three metrics at this knee, taken twice: **dVMAF closes on
all three goals** (-0.46 / -0.42 / -0.42 against a 0.5 bar, from -0.61 / -0.56
/ -0.56, reproducing to the decimal), **both worst-clip metrics hold** (goal 1
1.09-1.10x, goal 2 1.14x, under the 1.15x bar), and the cost is **2-4 points of
MEDIAN** (goal 1 0.96x -> 1.02-1.03x, goal 2 1.00x -> 1.00-1.04x).

So the arm does not fit a table at 1.05x median and 1.14x max, but the price is
quantified: 2-4 points of median for the whole dVMAF metric.

## Where the arm's remaining wall goes (`Y264_BPROF`, bus CIF, as-shipped t1)

The starting point for anyone sent to recover those table points. At `QGATE=6`
the B tournament goes **148.0 -> 173.3 ms, +25.3 ms**, and it is **not spread**:

| stage (INTER verdict) | default | arm | delta |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| **satd-phase** | 3.2 | **19.4** | **+16.2** |
| intra | 5.8 | 10.5 | +4.7 |
| rd-surv | 21.9 | 24.9 | +3.0 |
| me / direct-rd / s4-tail | 25.8 / 12.0 / 15.2 | 25.9 / 12.1 / 15.4 | ~0 |

Adding the SKIP and DIRECT verdicts' satd-phase growth (+1.6, +0.9),
**satd-phase is ~74% of the arm's entire cost**: `search_b_8x8`'s eight
quadrant searches plus the rectangular estimates.

**Do not look for an implementation gap there; there isn't one.** x264's
the B 8x8 analysis runs the same shape, a full motion search per
quadrant per list seeded with one candidate (the 16x16 result), so we are
structurally at their cost, which is what the two-sided price table already
said from the outside.

**The lever is the POPULATION, not the search.** At `QGATE=6` the quadrant
search runs on 24.1% of B macroblocks and the RD trial that follows beats the
running best on 4.5-11% of the trials it runs, so the great majority of the
searches we pay for lose. A gate that predicts "B_8x8 will actually win here"
better than four `satd8x8` of the winning 16x16 prediction would cut the
searched set at equal quality, and that is worth more than any
micro-optimisation of the search itself. Note the counter-evidence before
starting: the same shape of admission argument on the B skip decision
underdelivered its dump-measured promise
(`docs/b-skip-decision-design.md`), so measure the predictor's precision on the
real population before building a gate on it.
