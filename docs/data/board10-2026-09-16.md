# The first 10-bit board, 2026-09-16 (item C3-10bit)

BD-rate on VMAF-NEG, our High 10 against x264's, at matched achieved bytes.
Reproduce with `scripts/board10.sh`; the clip list and its rate targets are
`CLIPS_P10` in `scripts/parity-clips.sh`.

There is no gate value on this board. It is the first reading the 10-bit path
has ever had, recorded so the next one has something to move.

## What was measured

Eight BVI-AOM sequences at 480x272, converted to `yuv420p10le` Y4M and staged
on the external disk (`/Volumes/seagate/media/_p10`). Both encoders are solved
onto the same five byte targets per clip and the BD is taken over the
resulting (bytes, VMAF-NEG) pairs, so the two CRF scales cancel
(`scripts/bd_at_rate.py`). Targets were read off our own curve at VMAF-NEG
60/70/79/87/93, which puts every point inside the 55-95 band; no solve
saturated at the search bounds. Both arms at `--threads 4`, both at their
default preset. Ours says nothing about ten bits on the command line -- the
Y4M `C` tag picks the library -- and x264 needs BOTH `--profile high10` and
`--output-depth 10`, since the profile alone constrains the stream and still
writes 8-bit samples.

## The control, which is the point

A median of +21% at 480x272 on 64-frame clips could be the content, the
resolution, the clip length or the harness rather than the depth. It is the
depth. The same two worst clips, downconverted to 8-bit and run through the
same harness at the same rate targets against 8-bit x264:

| clip | 10-bit | 8-bit control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BusyHongKongStreetVidevo | +45.61% | **+3.48%** |
| JockeyHarmonics | +59.60% | **+0.32%** |

Same content, same resolution, same 64 frames, same five targets, same
solver, same metric. At 8 bits we are where the rest of the boards say we
are; at 10 we are not.

The control clips are staged beside the p10 ones as
`<clip>_p8.y4m` (`ffmpeg -i <bvi-aom source> -pix_fmt yuv420p`), and the arms
are our default binary against plain `x264`, no depth flags on either side:

```sh
python3 scripts/bd_at_rate.py \
  --a "build/cli/yah264 --input-y4m {src} --crf {q} --threads 4 -o {out}" \
  --b "x264 --crf {q} --threads 4 --log-level none -o {out} {src}" \
  --clip BusyHongKongStreetVidevo \
  --src /Volumes/seagate/media/_p10/BusyHongKongStreetVidevo_p8.y4m \
  --targets 106981,135762,195130,325106,555574 --label-a yah264-8 --label-b x264-8
```

PSNR says it too, so it is not a VMAF-at-10-bits artefact. On
BusyHongKongStreetVidevo at the board's middle rung, ours codes 179 881 bytes
at 28.37 dB luma and x264 codes **194 221** bytes -- 8% MORE -- at 30.66 dB.
Correcting for the byte gap still leaves about 1.9 dB.

## Where to look

Nothing in the coding tools is depth-specific: the same transform, prediction,
entropy coding and loop filter run at both widths, which is why a gap this
size is a defect rather than a property of High 10. What IS depth-dependent is
the arithmetic around them, and that is the list to work down:

- the QP domain. `QpBdOffsetY` is 12 at ten bits and the QP range shifts to
  [-12, 51]; anything that treats QP as an index into a table calibrated at
  8-bit, or clamps it at 0, is coding at the wrong operating point.
- the lambda tables and the RD cost scale, which key off that QP.
- the rate-control constants, whose bit-per-complexity models were fitted on
  8-bit measurements.
- the quant/dequant tables and the deadzone, whose rounding was chosen against
  an 8-bit residual range.

A cheap first cut is a fixed-QP cell: if the byte-vs-quality relation is sane
at a pinned QP and only the CRF sweep is wrong, the defect is in rate control;
if it is wrong at fixed QP too, it is in lambda or the quant path.

## Two caveats on quoting this board

The clips are **480x272**, the only BVI-AOM resolution class staged locally,
and **64 frames**, because the dataset's sequences are 64 frames by
construction and contain no scene cut. Sixty-four is below this project's own
">= 120 frames" rule for a BD sweep. Both are recorded here rather than
papered over; the 8-bit control shares them exactly, which is what lets the
comparison carry weight even though neither arm is at the shape the other
boards use.

## A harness bug found on the way, and fixed

`bdcompare.vmaf_of` decoded the bitstream to Y4M without `-fps_mode
passthrough`. An Annex-B stream carries its rate in the VUI, the Y4M muxer
settles on one of its own, and where the two disagree ffmpeg DROPS frames to
fit. The two 120 fps clips decoded **15 of their 64 frames**, silently, and
libvmaf scored those 15 against the first 15 of the reference: VMAF-NEG 16 at
qp 4, which reads as an encoder that cannot code the clip. Passthrough can
neither drop nor duplicate, so it is right wherever the rates already agreed
too. The 8-bit board clips were checked and were never affected (ducks_720p
at 50 fps decodes all 120 frames either way), so no published number moves.

## The dispatch's own cost, measured

The item routes every library call through a table of function pointers and
links both archives into one binary, so the 8-bit path had to be timed before
and after. Nine interleaved rounds per cell, mirrored launch order, output to
`/dev/null`, `--threads 1`, `perf_counter`, on a box with no other encoder,
ffmpeg, vmaf or ninja alive (1-minute load 4.05 falling to 2.84):

| cell | pre-item | new | ratio (median) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| foreman_cif, 280f | 0.767 s | 0.769 s | 1.0031x |
| park_joy_720p, 100f | 3.145 s | 3.162 s | 1.0055x |

The new binary is slower in 15 of the 18 pairs, so the sign is real, but the
size sits inside the same-binary run-to-run spread (0.3-2.9%) and it does NOT
scale with the CLI's share of the work: the CIF cell, where the CLI is
proportionally busiest, shows the smaller number. The dispatch fires once per
frame at most and cannot cost tenths of a percent. Binary layout is the
likelier cause -- the CLI links both archives now. Recorded as +0.3-0.6%
unattributed and within spread rather than as a clean null, because it is not
one.
